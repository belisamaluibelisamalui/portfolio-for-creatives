Experimental Type

Mend is an educational and studio program that offers workshops in woodworking and metalworking, enabling women, transgender, and non-binary individuals who have been formerly incarcerated to learn these crafts in an accessible setting. The purpose of the project is to establish a safe and inclusive environment where individuals can gain confidence, learn practical arts, and express themselves through the process of making something by hand.

Material & Structure

The visual direction for Mend draws inspiration from the raw materials and processes of woodworking and metalworking. Organic textures, weathered surfaces, and industrial references reflect the hands-on nature of the workshop, while structured grids, geometric forms, and bold typography communicate stability and precision. A palette of neutral tones, charcoal and earthy accents brings together the strength of metal and the warmth of wood.

Material & Structure

Typeface exploration was inspired by the organic shapes of tree burls, whose growth and round forms gave rise to letterforms. Sketches explored fluid shapes and curves, representing nature's imperfections and unique wood forms in typographic form. Proportions, curves, and the structure of letterforms were experimented with to create an interesting and unique typeface that would represent the connection of Mend with craftsmanship and materials.

Repair Tags

Designed for Mend's community repair service, the hangtags identify and organize customer items while providing clear communication between the workshop and its customers. Incorporating Mend's visual identity reinforces the connection between hands-on learning, repair services, and community engagement.
